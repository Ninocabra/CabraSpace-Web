Solar eclipse, twelfth of August

The 12 August 2026 total eclipse from the Cabrero viewpoint (Teruel, Spain): the corona (Fuji X-T5, HDR with CabraEclipse) and a timelapse of 2,193 photographs with the Vaonis Vespera Pro.

On 12 August 2026, at dusk, the Moon's shadow crossed the peninsula from west to east1: it was the first total eclipse visible from the Iberian Peninsula in more than a century2. For Fortanete and Villarroya de los Pinares, between which lies the Cabrero viewpoint7, the IGN calculated totality between 20:30:31 and 20:32:11 peninsular time, 1 min 40 s, with the Sun 5° above the horizon1. The solar cycle had already passed its maximum: the smoothed sunspot average peaked in October 2024, at 160.9, and the August 2026 average was 76.0, still provisional3. Predictive Science published a prediction of the corona4 with a model that assimilates the solar magnetic field in near real time, already tested at the 2024 eclipse5. According to the press, the Raman Research Institute's model got the large streamers right and missed a finer one, near the south pole6. There is no peer-reviewed study yet4.

My Vespera recorded only noise between 18:30:30 and 18:32:30 UTC7.

Notes

From the Cabrero viewpoint, between Fortanete and Villarroya de los Pinares (Teruel, Spain), with two instruments at the same spot. The Vaonis Vespera Pro photographed the Moon crossing the Sun roughly every 2 s, from first contact until sunset. During totality, between 18:30:30 and 18:32:30 UTC, its solar filter lets only noise through; then the crescent reappears on the other side and the Sun sets, still eclipsed, at about 18:51.

The corona (Fig. 1, and the 18:31 disc in the phase series) comes from the other camera: a handheld Fuji X-T5 at 300 mm focal length. It is 99 frames at different exposures over 25 s, merged into a single high-dynamic-range image with CabraEclipse, so that the faint outer corona and the edge of the Moon show at the same time.

All the phase discs are cut the same way, centred on the Sun, and painted in the same colour so the series reads at a glance.
