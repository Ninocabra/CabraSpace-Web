Transit of WASP-59 b

Light curve of the WASP-59 b exoplanet transit, measured from the AstroCamp observatory.

WASP-59 b, published in 2013, orbits a K5 dwarf every 7.9 days1. It is a warm Jupiter2 of 0.86 Jupiter masses and 0.78 Jupiter radii, probably with a large, dense core1. Its literature fits in a few lines3: two measurements of the angle between its orbit and the star's spin give 16° and −1°, with errors of almost 20°; the second is considered compatible with an aligned orbit2, 4. Gaia also found a companion star at about 9500 astronomical units5. My transit, centred on 4 September at 01:09 UTC, arrived 1.2 min late6, 7.

Notes

Partial result. The mid-transit time is good for O−C; the depth is not, because there was a meridian flip in the middle of the transit.
