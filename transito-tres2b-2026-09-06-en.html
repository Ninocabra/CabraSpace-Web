Transit of TrES-2 b

Light curve of the TrES-2 b exoplanet transit, measured from the AstroCamp observatory.

TrES-2 b was the first transiting planet found in the field of the future Kepler mission: a hot Jupiter that completes an orbit in 2.47 days, with transits 1.4% deep1. It crosses the star very close to its edge; in 2007 it had, among the nearby planets, the largest impact parameter2. In 2011 it was called “the darkest exoplanet known”3. According to the analysis of the Kepler data, its geometric albedo ranges from 0.0144 to 0.025, and it would fall below 1% if its thermal emission is discounted3. Its Hubble spectrum shows no features5. Whether its orbit decays remains open: with amateur transits (ETD database) a shortening of the period of 12.6 ± 2.4 ms per year was estimated6 and with TESS, of 5.58 ± 1.81 ms per year7, with neither regarded as conclusive; ExoClock IV (a 2025 preprint, not yet peer reviewed) does not include it among the planets that depart from a constant period8.

My night of 6 September began halfway through the transit: I have the egress, not the ingress9, inf.. The mid-transit time is valid, +1.8 ± 6.2 min against the ExoClock ephemeris; the depth is not9, 10.

Notes

Partial result. The mid-transit time is good for O−C; the depth is not, because the ingress of the transit is missing.
