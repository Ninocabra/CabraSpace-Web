Tabula II · Pl. IX · Light curve · two nights combined

Transit of WASP-52 b

Light curve of the WASP-52 b exoplanet transit, measured from the AstroCamp observatory.

WASP-52 b, discovered with SuperWASP and published in 2013, has half the mass of Jupiter and a radius 27% larger: an “inflated”, low-density planet1. Its transit is deep, 2.7%1. The star, an active K dwarf5, is dotted with spots that the planet sometimes covers as it passes: spot crossings were seen in 5 of 13 light curves2, and its brightness varies by about 7% peak to peak in the visible3. With Keck, helium was detected escaping from the planet: 3.44% of extra absorption, equivalent to losing 0.5% of its mass every billion years4. JWST has measured water vapour and helium, with two clear spot crossings in the same curve5. In 2026 came the first detection of carbon dioxide, although clouds and spots prevent pinning down its metallicity6.

On my two nights, 7 and 21 September, the mid-transit arrived 0.7 and 2.0 min earlier than the ExoClock ephemeris predicts7. The depth is not valid: there was a meridian flip in the middle of both transits7.

Notes

Two nights fitted together with a shared geometry. Each night gives its own transit time, which is sent separately to ExoClock.

Rp/R★ indicative. On the night of 7 Sep the depth is not good because there was a meridian flip in the middle of the transit, and the joint fit includes it. The transit times are good.

Rp/R★ indicative. On the night of 21 Sep the depth is not good because there was a meridian flip in the middle of the transit, and the joint fit includes it. The transit times are good.
