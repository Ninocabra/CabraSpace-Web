Transit of TrES-3 b

Light curve of the TrES-3 b exoplanet transit, measured from the AstroCamp observatory.

TrES-3 b was announced in 2007: 1.92 Jupiter masses and one orbit around its star every 1.31 days, about 31 hours, one of the shortest periods known at the time, ideal for studying whether its orbit decays1. It passes almost grazing the edge of the star, and that geometry is deceptive: a search of 330 planets with TESS attributed to it an apparent variation in its depth2. Whether the orbit is shrinking is under discussion. A 2024 analysis finds that the transit times fit a constant period, with no decay3; the ExoClock IV catalogue (a 2025 preprint, not yet peer reviewed) instead detects a long-term drift, which is absorbed by a quadratic term4. In its atmosphere, a rise towards the blue too large to be Rayleigh scattering5 turned out not to belong to the system; the data favour a flat spectrum6. In 2010 it was described as the first highly irradiated planet without a temperature inversion7.

My two nights agree with each other and give Rp/R★ = 0.1663 ± 0.0060, in agreement with the published value, 0.1655 ± 0.0020; the mid-transits arrived 3.2 and 1.0 min ahead of the ExoClock ephemeris8, 9. Two nights are not enough to speak of variationsinf..

Notes

Two nights fitted together with a shared geometry. Each night gives its own transit time, which is sent separately to ExoClock.
