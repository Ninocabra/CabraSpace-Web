Transit of Qatar-3 b

Light curve of the Qatar-3 b exoplanet transit, measured from the AstroCamp observatory.

Qatar-3 b is a “heavy hot Jupiter”: 4.31 ± 0.47 Jupiter masses with a radius similar to Jupiter's, around a young star, between 100 and 300 million years old1. Hardly anyone has studied it since: in arXiv abstracts only its discovery paper appears2, which did not even give the uncertainty of the period3. It is followed mainly by the ExoClock network, with 36 of the 46 recorded observations4. With the ExoClock ephemeris, my transit arrived 7.5 ± 4.4 min earlier than predicted4, 5; with other ephemerides that figure ranges from +7 to −18 mininf..

Notes

Partial result. The mid-transit time is good for O−C; the depth is not, because there was a meridian flip in the middle of the transit.
